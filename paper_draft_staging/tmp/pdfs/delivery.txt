How Does Chain-of-Thought Help Language Models Count?
A Mechanistic Comparison of Direct and Trace-Based Computation
Authors to be added
Working paper for an ICLR 2027 submission

Draft of July 29, 2026
Abstract
Counting all matching records in a long context requires more than retrieval: a model must
classify candidate records, cover the complete target set, aggregate their contributions, decide
when the computation is complete, and convert the result into an exact integer. Chain-of-thought
(CoT) often improves this task, but the source of the improvement is unknown. It may rescue
target retrieval, replace a difficult high-fan-in aggregation with repeated source-specific retrieval,
provide an external token memory, stabilize the final numeric readout, or merely add computation.
We study these alternatives in paired direct and trace-generating conditions. A behavioral audit
currently covers 26,500 realistic needle-in-a-haystack requests across target count, context length,
prompting mode, and model family. We propose two candidate computation graphs. Direct
answering may form one late, compressed total from a broad source-set routing path. CoT may
instead iterate a step-specific pointer, write retrieved evidence into the trace, update coverage
and termination state, and construct the final total from the resulting ledger. We define every
stage by its causal input–output behavior rather than by probe accuracy or attention mass, and
we specify interventions that test whether the trace-mediated path explains the paired CoT
accuracy gain beyond matched extra-compute controls. One-seed synthetic transformers show
that the proposed routing and state dissociations are possible; causal validation in realistic Qwen
and Gemma models remains a registered experimental target. [TODO: Replace the final
status sentence with preregistered paired-mode effect sizes, mechanism-mediated

Introduction

Long-context benchmarks usually ask whether a model can retrieve one item from distractors.
Counting changes the computational requirement: every valid record must contribute exactly once.
A successful system must solve five linked subproblems,
predicate evaluation → source routing → evidence accumulation
→ termination → numeric readout.

(1)

A single-needle score can conceal failures in any of the last four stages. Multi-needle tasks such as
RULER therefore provide a more diagnostic aggregation setting [5].
CoT improves many multi-step tasks [14], but an emitted explanation need not reveal the computation
that produced it [11]. Our central question is consequently not whether a longer response is more
accurate, but:
1

How does CoT improve language-model counting: by retrieving more targets, by restructuring aggregation, by stabilizing the final readout, or only by providing additional
autoregressive computation?
Our main hypothesis is a change in computational schedule. A direct answer must compress evidence
from many source records before emitting any assistant-side memory. A trace-generating model can
instead issue a source-specific query, write one retrieved record into the context, condition the next
forward pass on that write, track coverage or termination, and only then produce the total. This
can transform a high-fan-in terminal problem into repeated low-fan-in decisions. It does not imply
that the trace is faithful, that the model maintains a scalar internal counter, or that every accuracy
gain is reasoning-specific. Those claims require separate interventions.
We therefore distinguish four competing explanations:
1. retrieval rescue: CoT increases unique-target coverage;
2. serial/externalized aggregation: CoT converts set-level compression into repeated retrieval
plus an external trace ledger and progress/termination control;
3. readout rescue: both modes form comparable totals, but CoT improves conversion to the
answer tokens;
4. compute-only: semantically empty extra tokens would provide the same benefit.
The paper is designed to identify which link mediates the paired accuracy difference, rather than
merely to catalog different probes or attention heads.
This working paper makes four contributions, with their present status stated explicitly:
1. We formalize Direct and CoT as observable computation interfaces, decompose counting into
aligned semantic stages, and define what it would mean for a mechanism to explain the CoT
benefit.
2. We consolidate a realistic benchmark over N ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 20, 30} needles and L ∈
{2, 3, 5, 10, 20}k tokens, separating numerical accuracy from parsing, formatting, and truncation.
3. We specify paired semantic-trace, dummy-compute, state/head/MLP patching, query-local
ablation, and restoration tests for Qwen3 and Gemma4 models that expose thinking and nonthinking modes [2, 15].
4. We use synthetic transformers to study when routing roles and count-state geometry emerge
during training. Existing results motivate the LLM experiments but do not substitute for them.
The strongest current evidence is behavioral: Index and Native conditions often outperform Direct
in the realistic audit. Earlier realistic reports and controlled synthetic models motivate the candidate
graph, but do not establish that a pretrained LLM uses it. The decisive upgrade is a mediation
result: selectively disrupting trace semantics or the trace-mediated path should remove the CoT
advantage, while restoring the registered intermediate state should recover it; length-, position-,
norm-, and compute-matched controls should not.

Operational mechanism definitions

Definition 4 (Validity evidence, ordinal, and pointer). A candidate state carries validity evidence
for Vi if it generalizes across source identity, template, token, and position holdouts, and a fixedordinal validity flip changes whether Ri contributes downstream. The source ordinal is candidate
P
index i, independent of validity. The target rank is j≤i Vj and equals the prefix count at a valid
target endpoint; it therefore cannot by itself identify a running counter. A pointer Pk identifies which
source is selected at trace step k and is tested by source-identity transport, not ordinal decoding
alone.
Definition 5 (Retrieval). A source record Ri is retrieved at semantic site q if a task-relevant
clean–corrupt change to Ri , with registered nuisances fixed, changes a downstream semantic state
or decision through a source-specific path and restoring that path restores the effect. Attention
probability is a discovery statistic, not sufficient evidence of retrieval.
Retrieval is broad when one update or path bundle has independently measurable causal support
on multiple valid records. It is targeted when support is concentrated on one intended record
and switches appropriately when a pointer or requested identity is changed. A Direct system can
implement broad coverage through several individually narrow or multi-hop paths; a CoT system
can retrieve one item at a time and later perform a set aggregation over the trace.
Definition 6 (Aggregation). An aggregation state composes retrieved validity contributions into a
statistic sufficient for the final count. It must change predictably when one source is added, deleted,
duplicated, or validity-flipped, and restoring the state must mediate that source’s effect on the final
answer. Parallel aggregation applies a set-level map before the answer; serial aggregation applies an
update across aligned trace steps. Decoding N alone is insufficient.
Definition 7 (Count code). A semantic site carries a count code if a decoder trained on disjoint
documents predicts the registered count above lexical, position-only, and shuffled-label controls on
held-out templates, lengths, target identities, and counts. A count code is a representational property;
it need not be read or updated by the model.
Definition 8 (Register and algorithmic counter). A state is a register for variable v if v is readable
under lexical and positional controls, transporting the state transports the corresponding downstream
decision, and the effect generalizes across held-out content and positions. It is an algorithmic counter
only if it additionally has an event-conditioned update law
g(F (s, e)) − g(s) ≈ e,

identities, and positions. A count code or a state correlated with a visible index is not a counter.
Candidate validity, source pointer, covered set, semantic progress, termination, and final total
are different register roles. In free-running traces, kemit , kvalid , kunique = Gk , and N − kunique are
retained separately. A progress claim is evaluated primarily against Gk , at fixed emitted step, item
validity, duplication, position, and index label.
Definition 9 (Final-total state). A pre-answer state is a final-total state if a same-position donor
transplant moves the complete parsed-count distribution with donor total after semantic total, trace
extent, final visible index, answer position, and source identity have been varied independently.
Decoding N , copying the last index, or following answer position alone is insufficient.

Definition 10 (Label-matched state comparison). Two sites are directly comparable for label L
only when they precede tokens revealing L, use the same semantic definition, expose the registered
upstream evidence, and factorially control token identity, absolute/relative position, and sequence
length. Direct and CoT pre-answer sites are comparable for final total N . Direct source-prefix count
C≤t and CoT semantic progress Gk are not a primary matched pair.
This distinction answers where to search for a non-thinking counter. The primary Direct site is the

positions test the stronger, separate hypothesis that a prefix state is carried during the prompt pass.
At natural valid target endpoints, target rank equals C≤t by construction; the test must instead fix
candidate ordinal while validity changes, hold the number of seen candidates fixed while the valid
prefix changes, and test persistence at matched filler boundaries.

Definition 11 (Operational mediator of the CoT benefit). A variable or path Z explains part of
the CoT benefit only if (i) CoT changes or introduces Z on paired prompts; (ii) locally disrupting
Z damages its registered intermediate decision and reduces the CoT advantage beyond compute-,
position-, norm-, and component-matched controls; and (iii) restoring clean Z restores both the
intermediate endpoint and the final count. A mode difference in probe accuracy, geometry, or
attention mass is insufficient.
Definition 12 (Mechanism equivalence). Output equivalence means that two conditions return
the same count; algorithmic equivalence means that both implement the same abstract sum; causalmechanistic equivalence additionally requires a role-preserving alignment of semantic states under
which every preregistered intervention produces matched downstream response vectors within a frozen
tolerance. Similar probes or geometry do not establish mechanism equivalence.

How CoT Could Help: Competing Mechanisms

Hypothesis 1 (Retrieval rescue). CoT increases unique valid-source coverage or reduces false
positives and duplicates. It predicts higher source-aligned recall before any final readout advantage.
It is redirected if traces are not source-faithful or if Direct and CoT have matched causal source
coverage after formatting and truncation are removed.
Hypothesis 2 (Serial and externalized aggregation). CoT changes the schedule from a high-fanin terminal aggregate to repeated targeted retrieval, an external trace ledger, coverage/progressdependent routing, and learned stopping. Semantic trace corruptions, source-pointer interventions,
and progress/termination interventions should selectively damage CoT; clean local patches should
restore the affected step and final total.
Hypothesis 3 (Final-state stabilization). Both modes may retrieve the relevant sources, while Direct
compresses their cardinality into a less discriminable final state. CoT predicts a better-separated ZC
and fewer final-readout errors after perfect source coverage. This is a final-total versus final-total
comparison; CoT progress is not compared with Direct total.
7

Hypothesis 4 (Compute-only alternative). The improvement may follow solely from extra generated
tokens and recurrent depth. If an equal-length semantically null scratchpad reproduces the gain, and
shuffling or corrupting trace content has no additional effect once length and position are fixed, the
evidence favors compute rather than semantic serial mediation.
Hypothesis 5 (Semantic-path mediation). If targeted retrieval and trace-mediated aggregation
explain the behavioral gain, disabling their registered source-pointer, external-memory, coverage/progress, termination, or trace-to-answer paths will remove a preregistered fraction of ∆CoT ,
and a clean patch will restore it. Dummy-token, random-path, and wrong-site patches will not.
These hypotheses are not mutually exclusive. CoT can improve coverage and final-state quality
while also consuming more compute. The experimental goal is to decompose the gain, not to force
every model into one universal circuit.

Behavioral Study and Model Set

4.1

Realistic NIAH counting

The current V2 audit contains 53 model–mode cells and 500 requests per cell (26,500 total). Each
complete cell crosses ten target counts, five context lengths, and ten seeds. A valid target is a
city–score audit record embedded among realistic distractors. The evaluation records four separate
outcomes:
8

1. numerical exactness of the parsed total;
2. parse success;
3. compliance with the strict final-answer format;
4. truncation before a valid answer.
Registered success requires all four gates. Enumeration precision/recall and trace style are diagnostics,
not part of the numerical target.
The existing dataset is adequate for behavioral description but not yet a clean causal comparison of
reasoning modes. Direct/index/bullet decoding is usually deterministic, whereas native thinking
currently uses model-specific temperatures and larger token budgets. Some families compare different
post-trained checkpoints rather than one checkpoint with a mode toggle. Therefore, all mechanistic
claims will use the same weights, identical input, matched decoding where possible, and a separately
reported compute/token-budget intervention.

Behavioral estimands and inference

For each request, signed count error is e = Cb − C. We report exact accuracy, conditional bias,
mean absolute and squared error, under/over-count probability, parse/format/truncation rates, and
conditional error variance. Primary comparisons are paired within the same generated context
and seed. The primary uncertainty procedure is a paired cluster bootstrap that resamples contextgeneration seeds within model and registered (N, L) cells; McNemar tests provide paired binary
checks. A discrete, heteroscedastic hierarchical error model with prespecified mode, N , L, and
interaction terms is secondary because the model-family count is small. The multiplicity family
covers primary modes, layers, and intervention doses declared before analysis. Model-generated
trace style is post-treatment and is reported descriptively rather than interpreted causally.

4.4

Mechanistic sample construction

Mechanistic examples are stratified by (N, L), output correctness, target placement, and trace
correctness. We use three trace regimes:
1. canonical teacher forcing, with machine-parseable item boundaries and a gold source alignment;
2. free-running native traces, preserving ecological validity and error propagation;
3. counterfactual traces, independently varying source count, trace extent, explicit index identity,
and final answer position.
Every free-running item is aligned to a source span or labeled hallucinated/unresolved; duplicates,
inline prose, and unparsable traces remain explicit categories. We report identifiable-trace coverage
10

and distinguish conditional aligned-trace estimands from all-rollout behavior. For progress, kemit ,
kvalid , kunique , and remaining count are retained separately.
Main mode comparisons include all paired examples on a common prompt distribution and report
four outcome strata: both correct, Direct-only correct, trace-mode-only correct, and both wrong.
Common-correct comparisons are selection-conditioned sensitivities. A prespecified subset of
overlapping-difficulty (N, L) cells supplies an iso-difficulty comparison; post-hoc accuracy matching
is not used as the primary estimate.

Supplementary Synthetic Bridge and Training Dynamics

Synthetic models permit exact source alignment, dense checkpoints, and interventions that are
prohibitively expensive in LLMs. Their role is to develop and falsify measurements, not to prove
that an LLM uses the same circuit.

7.1

What one-seed v10 supports in its own setting

Synthetic v10 trains separate 4-layer, 4-head, dmodel = 256 causal transformers on counts 1–30 with
learned absolute positions. Within this toy setting it provides five transferable lessons:
17

1. total count is highly linearly readable at the final query in both modes, but adjacent-count
geometry bends and becomes higher-dimensional with depth;
2. direct answer queries use early broad-routing heads, while trace queries use targeted multi-head
bundles;
3. progress/termination and final-total states are causally separable;
4. local count transport follows a curved centroid path better than one global +1 direction;
5. readout attention mass alone does not imply that selected head slices carry the full scalar state.
For example, direct broad-head query-local ablation can collapse accuracy, whereas targeted tracemodel heads require a bundle to recover source identity. At the final residual, donor count
is transported almost perfectly; a final trace-marker state instead controls early close without
transporting the final scalar. These results satisfy parts of the causal evidence ladder in a toy
model, but v10 uses one seed, symbolic markers, fixed short contexts, separately trained models,
and teacher forcing for many analyses.

coverage; trace queries show targeted source retrieval; final trace readout is a separate candidate
role. These are descriptive rankings and require local interventions for causal naming.

7.2

v15 and v20 as candidate final settings

Version 15 uses Tiny Shakespeare backgrounds, dmodel = 256, RoPE or learned relative bias, allsequence loss, and inserted marker targets. It changes several factors simultaneously and therefore
serves as a negative design result rather than a clean ablation. Final counts approach ceiling
even when trace fidelity collapses: RoPE trace exactness across low/mid/high count bands is
approximately 68/49/17%, and relative-bias trace exactness is 24/0/0%, while final counts remain
essentially perfect. Position-only controls at key trace/final sites can also reach ceiling. Thus correct
answers do not establish faithful trace aggregation, and high trace-site probes can be positional.
Version 20 counts the union occurrences of a queried set of three natural characters in a 256-character
Tiny Shakespeare window, restricted to totals 1–30. It uses a query-first format, RoPE, atomic count
tokens, paired data/order for separately trained direct and trace models, and a two-stage objective:
all-sequence loss through step 1500, then task-output loss through step 10,000. Both models have
four layers, four heads, and dmodel = 256. At the final checkpoint (one seed, 50 held-out free-running
examples per count), the trace model reaches .912 exact count (Wilson 95% CI [.897,.925]), .888
whole-trace exactness, and .983 ordered-marker accuracy. The direct model reaches .335 exact count
(CI [.311,.359]), with MAE 1.177. Its output error does not support the simplest monotonic-noise

Training-dynamics estimands

For checkpoints every 100–500 updates, we track free-running exact accuracy, teacher-forced
local accuracy, source-routing selectivity, progress and total-state discriminability, local ablation
damage, and patch recovery. Training step is supplemented by semantic exposure: the number of
supervised occurrences of each retrieval step k, target identity, and answer token. In the current one
seed, successor behavior appears first, teacher-forced final readout follows, and targeted retrieval
specializes gradually around steps 3,300–6,000; free-running trace accuracy rises later. This ordering
is descriptive because checkpoints are autocorrelated and the objective changes at step 1,500.
We distinguish three possibilities:

2. phase transition: a preregistered change-point model improves held-out likelihood/BIC and

3. objective artifact: the transition disappears, moves, or reverses when the loss switch and
semantic exposure are controlled.

Threats to Causal Identification

Retrieval failure versus aggregation failure. High list recall with low bare-count accuracy,
length dilution at fixed N , and direct-state steering are compatible with an aggregation/readout
bottleneck. Nevertheless, source retrieval must be measured with the same prompt and decoding as
counting. Target mass can fall merely because attention is normalized over longer contexts, and a
multi-hop summary path can lack direct answer-to-source attention.
Visible-index leakage. Emitted-step progress can be decoded trivially from an index token or
trace position. The main analysis uses pre-index/item-end anchors, symbolic index permutations,
index-free traces, and fixed-step validity/duplication counterfactuals to separate kemit from semantic
kunique .
Position and trace length. Answer position often grows with trace extent. We cross trace
extent with answer position: add padding to change position at fixed extent, and compensate with

20

padding to fix answer position while extent changes. Patch tables retain donor/receiver positions,
relative distance, and rotary phase.
Conditioning on correct traces. Correct-only traces select easier examples and can manufacture
a clean manifold. Main estimates use all runs and stratify by trace correctness; canonical teacher
forcing is labeled as a local competence test.
Capability mismatch. Direct and trace-generating modes currently have different accuracy, so a
circuit difference may reflect success versus failure rather than computation style. Primary estimates
use all paired prompts and the four paired correctness strata; preregistered overlapping-difficulty
cells provide a secondary comparison. Common-correct and post-hoc iso-accuracy subsets are not
treated as unbiased mode effects.
Different compute and decoding. Trace-generating modes use more generated tokens and
sometimes a different temperature. We report a native-policy effect, a matched-decoding effect, and
an explicit token-budget curve. Different post-trained checkpoints are not treated as a reasoningmode toggle.
Attention superposition and redundancy. One semantic role may be distributed across heads,
one head may be polysemantic, and a set of narrow heads may collectively provide broad coverage.
Bundle/path-level add-delete tests, dose-response patching, and query-local ablation are preferred
to naming the single highest-scoring head.
Off-manifold interventions. Large centroid replacements can create unnatural activations. We
report activation norm, nearest-neighbor distance to natural states, downstream KL, and compare
residual-preserving deltas with full replacement.

Related Work

CoT prompting improves performance on multi-step tasks [14], but trace faithfulness is not guaranteed [11]. Recent work analyzes information flow and local causal structure inside reasoning
traces [13, 16]; the present task adds exact source alignments and a known algorithmic target. Work
on hidden error signals further cautions that a strong probe can be diagnostic but not causal [18].
RULER extends single-needle retrieval to multi-needle and aggregation tasks [5]. Theoretical and
empirical work shows that transformer counting can be limited by representation interference,
vocabulary, and context length [17]. Our focus is complementary: we compare routing and stateupdate mechanisms across response modes in pretrained language models and controlled synthetic
transformers.
Mechanistic interpretability provides the intervention tools used here: causal tracing and activation
patching [7], circuit discovery [1], query-specific circuit analysis [12], and activation steering [10].
Successor heads implement local increments over ordered token domains [3]; we distinguish such a
primitive from the broader retrieval–aggregation algorithm required by long-context counting.

10

Limitations

The realistic mechanistic evidence currently comes from heterogeneous report generations, prompts,
decoding policies, and sample sizes. The strongest direct-mode evidence is not yet replicated in the
V2 setting, and the strongest trace-mechanism evidence is synthetic. Open-weight Qwen/Gemma
results may not generalize to proprietary or non-autoregressive systems. Linear probes can miss
nonlinear or distributed codes, while powerful nonlinear probes can memorize nuisances. Patching
can be off-manifold and ablation can trigger compensation. Synthetic models differ from LLMs
in scale, pretraining, tokenization, and task familiarity. Finally, the law analysis is exploratory:
candidate structures were selected after observing the data and must be validated on new model
sizes or new condition cells.

Conclusion

The paper asks not merely whether CoT and Direct produce different activations, but which
change in computation explains the CoT counting advantage. Our candidate answer is a scheduling
transformation: Direct must route and compress a source set before producing any assistant-side
memory, whereas CoT can repeatedly issue a source-specific query, write evidence into an external
ledger, condition later retrieval and stopping on that ledger or an internal coverage state, and only
then form the final total. This account remains a hypothesis in realistic LLMs. It will be supported
only if the registered semantic path is present on generated prefixes, its disruption removes part of
the paired accuracy gain, and clean restoration recovers both the intermediate transition and the
count beyond equal-compute controls. Retrieval-only, readout-only, and compute-only outcomes
remain live alternatives. This intervention-centered criterion turns the behavioral observation that
CoT helps counting into a falsifiable mechanistic claim.

Statistical Specification for the Noise Hypothesis

For mode m, site s, layer ℓ, and count c, fit a grouped cross-validated decoder on training groups
only. On held-out groups define
bmsℓ (c) = E[Cb − C | c],

vmsℓ (c) = Var(Cb − C | c),

qmsℓ (c) = vmsℓ (c) + bmsℓ (c)2 .

(20)

On a dense C = 1:30 mechanistic subset, estimate separate slopes for log(v + ϵ) and log(q + ϵ)
against log c while controlling for context length, target-depth distribution, trace length, answer
position, and prespecified difficulty cell. Representation-level analysis uses the pairwise, countgap-normalized metrics in equation (17); sparse-grid analyses regress them on interval midpoint
rather than pretending c + 1 was observed. The primary contrast is Direct-final-total versus
Native-final-total, with Direct versus Index reported separately. Prefix/progress comparisons are
secondary because they label different computational objects. Cluster bootstrap intervals resample
documents and seeds; Brown–Forsythe and shrinkage-Mahalanobis analyses are sensitivities. The
claim requires convergent conditional-dispersion, MSE, and representation-discriminability evidence,
not one significant slope.
