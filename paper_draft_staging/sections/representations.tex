\section{Counter Representations and Causal State Control}
\label{sec:representations}

\subsection{Two minimal computation graphs}

The behavioral gap leaves open where the total is represented and whether a model updates it sequentially. We therefore begin with two minimal computation graphs, without assigning individual heads or neurons in advance.

\paragraph{Direct: broad retrieval followed by a terminal total.}
Let \(\rho_i^D\) be the task-relevant contribution retrieved from record \(R_i\). The Direct candidate is
\begin{equation}
\rho_i^D=\mathcal R_D(x,R_i,v_i;q_D),
\qquad
Z_D=G_D(\rho_1^D,\ldots,\rho_M^D),
\qquad
\widehat C_D=Q_D(Z_D).
\label{eq:direct-mechanism}
\end{equation}
Here \(q_D\) is the position immediately before the first answer digit. ``Broad'' means that multiple valid records have independently measurable causal effects through a common downstream route or route bundle. It does not require uniform attention or one globally broad head. ``Terminal'' means that the causally sufficient total \(Z_D\) is constructed near the answer; it does not deny that earlier prompt states contain partial information.

\paragraph{CoT: targeted retrieval followed by iterative aggregation.}
At trace step \(k\), let \(\pi_k\) identify the intended source, \(e_k\) the written item, and \(\Gamma_k\) the aggregate carrier formed by an optional internal state together with the visible trace and its KV cache. The CoT candidate is
\begin{align}
\pi_k &= \Pi_C(x,\Gamma_{k-1}),
&
e_k &= W_C\!\left(\mathcal R_C(x,R_{\pi_k};q_k^{\rm ret})\right),\\
\Gamma_k &= F_C(\Gamma_{k-1},e_k),
&
K &= \min\{k:\sigma_C(\Gamma_k)=\textsc{stop}\},\\
Z_C &= G_C(\Gamma_K),
&
\widehat C_C &= Q_C(Z_C).
\label{eq:cot-mechanism}
\end{align}
The retrieval query \(q_k^{\rm ret}\) occurs immediately before the source-bearing content of item \(k\); \(q_k^{\rm end}\) is the item end before the next-item or close decision; \(q_C\) is immediately before the first final-answer digit. ``Targeted'' means that the causal source contribution at step \(k\) is concentrated on \(R_{\pi_k}\) and retargets under a counterfactual change of \(\pi_k\). ``Iterative aggregation'' means that changing a semantically new, duplicate, or invalid item changes the later carrier, next retrieval, stopping decision, or final total in the predicted way. The visible trace may be the memory; an internal scalar counter is not assumed.

\subsection{Registered count states}

At layer \(\ell\), we register only three residual-stream states:
\begin{equation}
Z_D^\ell=h^\ell(q_D),
\qquad
P_{C,k}^\ell=h^\ell(q_k^{\rm end}),
\qquad
Z_C^\ell=h^\ell(q_C).
\label{eq:registered-states}
\end{equation}
\(Z_D^\ell\) and \(Z_C^\ell\) are candidate \emph{final-total representations} of the same variable \(C\). \(P_{C,k}^\ell\) is a candidate \emph{progress state} whose semantic label is the number \(k_{\rm unique}\) of distinct valid sources covered so far, not the emitted item number or the last visible numeral. State quality is compared across modes only at the matched final-total sites \(Z_D^\ell\) and \(Z_C^\ell\); a Direct total is never compared with a CoT progress state.

This distinction resolves where to search for a non-thinking counter. We first test for a total code at \(q_D\) across prompts with different \(C\). We then scan states after candidate records and matched filler boundaries. A prompt-side state is called an \emph{iterative counter} only if one new valid record causes a \(+1\) update, an invalid or already-counted record causes a no-op, the update persists, and transplanting it changes the final count. If these event-level tests fail while \(Z_D^\ell\) remains causally transportable, the Direct mechanism contains a terminal total representation but no demonstrated prompt-side counter. Cross-prompt decoding at \(q_D\) alone cannot answer this question.

For CoT, let
\[
u_k=
\begin{cases}
1,& e_k\text{ covers a new valid source},\\
0,& e_k\text{ is invalid or duplicates a covered source}.
\end{cases}
\]
A component of \(P_{C,k}^\ell\) is an iterative counter only if a held-out semantic readout \(g\) satisfies
\begin{equation}
g(P_{C,k}^\ell)-g(P_{C,k-1}^\ell)\approx u_k
\label{eq:counter-update}
\end{equation}
and intervention on that component changes a downstream retrieval, stopping decision, or final count. If only trace-token or KV-cache patches transport progress, the supported mechanism is an external trace ledger rather than an internal residual counter.

\subsection{Counter curves and representation quality}

Linear decoding asks whether count information is present; it does not describe geometry or causal use. Let \(z\) collect preregistered nuisances such as context length, answer position, target-depth statistics, trace length, and local token identity. Residualize the registered state using a fit split,
\[
\widetilde h_s^\ell
=h_s^\ell-\widehat{\E}[h_s^\ell\mid z],
\]
and define the held-out count centroid
\begin{equation}
\mu_s^\ell(c)=\E[\widetilde h_s^\ell\mid C=c].
\label{eq:count-centroid}
\end{equation}
The \emph{counter curve} at site \(s\) and layer \(\ell\) is the ordered sequence of full-space centroids together with adjacent displacements
\begin{equation}
\mathcal K_s^\ell=
\left(
\mu_s^\ell(c_1),\ldots,\mu_s^\ell(c_J);
\delta_j^\ell=\mu_s^\ell(c_{j+1})-\mu_s^\ell(c_j)
\right).
\label{eq:counter-curve}
\end{equation}
A two-dimensional PCA plot is only \(\Pi_2\mathcal K_s^\ell\): the basis is fitted on the discovery split and held fixed for reporting data. Ordering or visual smoothness in PCA is descriptive and does not show that the model traverses the curve during computation.

We use ``noise'' only as shorthand for conditional representation dispersion across matched prompts, not stochasticity of a deterministic hidden state. For adjacent observed counts, define
\begin{align}
t_j^\ell
&=
\frac{\mu^\ell(c_{j+1})-\mu^\ell(c_j)}
{\|\mu^\ell(c_{j+1})-\mu^\ell(c_j)\|_2},
&
d_j^\ell
&=
\frac{\|\mu^\ell(c_{j+1})-\mu^\ell(c_j)\|_2}{c_{j+1}-c_j},\\
\overline\Sigma_j^\ell
&=
\frac{\Cov(\widetilde h^\ell\mid c_j)+
\Cov(\widetilde h^\ell\mid c_{j+1})}{2},
&
\SNR_j^\ell
&=
\frac{(d_j^\ell)^2}
{(t_j^\ell)^\top\overline\Sigma_j^\ell t_j^\ell+\epsilon}.
\label{eq:state-snr}
\end{align}
\(\SNR_j^\ell\) compares unit-count centroid spacing with within-count dispersion in the local count direction. We report the two terms separately, because a low SNR may arise from smaller spacing, larger dispersion, or both. Normal-direction dispersion, shrinkage Mahalanobis distance, decoder bias/variance, and nuisance sensitivity are appendix analyses.

\begin{todobox}
\textbf{R1---counter-curve map.}
For each co-primary model and Direct/CoT mode, collect the dense 4k mechanism set and sparse 8k replication. At every layer, extract \(Z_D^\ell\), \(P_{C,k}^\ell\), and \(Z_C^\ell\); fit nuisance regressions, PCA bases, and linear decoders on discovery documents only; evaluate on held-out documents, identities, positions, and counts. \subsection{Geometric steering and natural-state patching}

To test whether the counter curve is executable, preserve the receiver-specific residual while moving only its count coordinate. For a receiver with count \(c_r\), residual \(\epsilon_r=h_r-\mu(c_r)\), and a point \(\gamma_{r\rightarrow d}(\alpha)\) on the piecewise path through observed centroids toward donor count \(c_d\), intervene with
\begin{equation}
h_r'(\alpha)=\epsilon_r+\gamma_{r\rightarrow d}(\alpha),
\qquad 0\leq\alpha\leq1.
\label{eq:path-steering}
\end{equation}
We compare this local path with the endpoint chord, norm-matched tangent-orthogonal directions, random directions, and full centroid replacement. The output endpoint is the complete parsed-count distribution or its expected value
\[
\mathcal C(h)=\sum_c c\,p(\widehat C=c\mid h),
\]
not the logit of the first digit.

A stronger test uses same-site natural donor states. For donor count \(c_d\) and receiver count \(c_r\), patch the donor residual or the minimal localized state slice into the receiver and measure the transport slope
\begin{equation}
\beta_{\rm patch}
=
\frac{\E[\mathcal C(h_{\rm patched})-\mathcal C(h_{\rm receiver})]}
{c_d-c_r}.
\label{eq:patch-slope}
\end{equation}
Bidirectional slopes near one, together with low off-manifold distance and ineffective matched random patches, support a causally sufficient total coordinate. Steering alone is weaker because an artificial direction may bypass the natural computation.

\begin{todobox}
\textbf{R2---geometry causality.}
Use disjoint state-fit and intervention prompts. For \(Z_D^\ell\), \(P_{C,k}^\ell\), and \(Z_C^\ell\), run bidirectional adjacent-count steering, non-adjacent curve-versus-chord steering, residual-preserving centroid movement, and same-position natural donor transplants. Balance \(\delta=\pm1,\pm2\), length, answer position, trace extent, and correctness stratum. Report transport slope, path-tracking error, adoption of the donor parsed count, activation distance to natural states, downstream KL, and complete free-running exactness. A causally executable count state requires a monotone bidirectional dose response, superiority to norm-matched orthogonal/random controls, and agreement between geometric and natural-state interventions.
\end{todobox}

\begin{todobox}
\textbf{R3---iterative counter versus terminal total.}
For Direct, create fixed-token-length record triples in which one candidate is new-valid, invalid, or a duplicate while candidate ordinal and local depth are fixed. Test the immediate record-end state, subsequent matched filler boundaries, and \(q_D\). For CoT, create item-prefix triples with the same visible item number and length but new-valid, invalid, or duplicate semantics; patch residual state and trace-token/KV state separately at \(q_k^{\rm end}\). Require \(+1\), no-op, persistence, held-out identity generalization, and a downstream retrieval/stop/final-answer effect before using ``iterative counter.'' \(q_D/q_C\)-only transport supports a final-total representation; trace/KV-only transport supports an external ledger.
\end{todobox}
